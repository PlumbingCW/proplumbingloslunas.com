Emergency Water Line Repair in Los Lunas, NM.

Emergency water line repair in Los Lunas, NM covers the pipe that carries water from the meter to your house. When it breaks, you may see a wet yard, a drop in pressure or no water at all. The 24/7 line takes the call and a plumber gives a clear price before work starts.

Signs your water service line is failing

  • A soggy patch or sinkhole in the yard with no rain.
  • A sudden drop in water pressure.
  • A meter that keeps running with all fixtures off.
  • Dirty or discolored water.
  • A higher bill with no change in use.

These signs can also match a slab leak or an in-house leak, so a quick test comes first. See slab leak repair for the in-house side.

Who owns which part of the line

In the Village of Los Lunas, the water meter is the dividing point in most cases. The Village Water Division maintains the system up to the meter. The line from the meter to your house is yours. If you are not sure where the break is, call the Village Water Division first to confirm the meter side.

Rio Communities is served by a private water company, so the contact there is different. In Peralta, most homes use private wells, so a well line is a separate question.

A line that has failed once may fail again nearby, especially if it is old steel or early plastic. A plumber will look at the pipe around the break and tell you what shape it is in.

Digging in the valley

Soils along the Rio Grande range from sandy to clay, and cottonwood roots can reach long distances for water. Roots can push on a line and crack a joint. Before any dig, New Mexico requires a call to locate underground utilities so crews do not hit gas or electric lines.

The plumber handles the locate request. Ask about restoration of lawn, gravel or paving so you know what the price includes.

Preventing the next break

Know where your main shutoff is and exercise it twice a year so it does not seize. Keep roots and heavy equipment away from the line path. Insulate exposed pipe and outdoor spigots before winter nights.

Check your water pressure now and then. Pressure far above normal stresses the line and the fixtures in the house. A pressure reducing valve is inexpensive protection when pressure runs high.

When no water is the whole problem

If every tap is dry, check whether neighbors are affected. A neighborhood outage points to the utility. If only your house is dry, check the main shutoff, then the meter. A shutoff partly closed by accident is more common than a break.

If the meter box is full of water or the ground around it is soft, call. The line between the meter and the house is likely the cause.

Water service line standards

A new water service line follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) for pipe material, depth and connections. Calling the New Mexico utility locate service before any dig is also standard, so crews do not strike buried gas or electric lines.
