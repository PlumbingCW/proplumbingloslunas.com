Start with the house, not the heater

The right heater depends on what the house already has. Is there a gas line near the heater location? How large is it? Is there a vent path to the roof or wall? Is there a 240 volt circuit with spare capacity? These answers decide which units are even possible.

Planning also lets you size the heater honestly. Count the people at home, the number of bathrooms and whether you run a soaking tub. A 40 gallon tank suits a couple. A family of five with two showers in the morning needs more storage or a tankless unit.

Tankless installs often involve a larger gas line and stainless or polypropylene venting. Those upgrades are why a tankless quote can look high next to a tank. The tankless water heater page walks through those line items.

Permits, code and who is allowed to install

In New Mexico the Construction Industries Division issues plumbing permits and inspects the work. The state code adopts the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code. A permit is not always needed, so confirm your scope when booking.

Water heater work falls under the MM-1 plumbing license. Gas appliance work falls under MM-2, which is why some jobs are done by a plumber who holds a combined classification. The state license lookup lets you check any contractor before they start.

Details that make an install last

  • A drain pan with a pipe to a safe drain location when the heater sits above finished space.
  • A temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge line.
  • Dielectric unions or an equivalent where copper meets steel.
  • A thermal expansion control device when the plumbing is a closed system.
  • Clear labeling of the shutoff valves for the next person who needs them.

None of these is exciting, but they are the items that turn a heater from a ten year unit into a fifteen year unit. Ask your plumber which ones are included in the quote.

Anode rods, flushing and the first year

Tank heaters use an anode rod to take the corrosion that would otherwise attack the steel. Checking it every few years extends the tank’s life. Flushing the tank once a year clears sediment before it bakes on.

If your tap water leaves white crust, ask the Village Water Division for its latest water quality report to see what minerals are present. That helps you decide whether a softener from the water softener and filtration page is worth considering.

Code requirements for a new install

New installations follow the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in New Mexico. Pay attention to clearances, the relief valve discharge, seismic or strap requirements where they apply and venting for gas units. A permit and inspection protect you at resale.
