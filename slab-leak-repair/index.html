Signs of a slab leak

  • A warm or hot spot on the floor that you can feel with bare feet.
  • A water meter that keeps moving when every fixture is off.
  • The sound of running water when nothing is on.
  • Damp carpet, curling flooring or a musty smell.
  • A sudden jump in the water bill.

A single sign can have other causes. Two or more together make a slab leak likely, and the next step is a test.

How a slab leak is located

The plumber isolates the water line and checks if pressure holds. Listening gear picks up the sound of the leak through the concrete. Thermal imaging can show a hot line. Tracer gas works when sound is weak.

Locating first matters because it limits cutting to the smallest area. See leak detection for more on each tool.

Which option fits

A single leak in otherwise sound copper is often a spot repair. A house with several leaks in a few years is a different case, because a repair fixes one point and the next weak point fails soon after.

If that sounds like your house, read about repiping before choosing. Rerouting is often a middle path.

Slab homes in Los Lunas, Belen and Bosque Farms

Many homes built on slabs in the valley run supply lines in or below the concrete. Soil moves with moisture and seasons, and pipes can rub on rock or gravel. Hard groundwater can also wear on metal pipe over time.

The Village of Los Lunas draws water from the Santa Fe Group aquifer through four wells. A leak on your side of the meter is your cost, so a quick test saves water and money.

Before we arrive: suspected slab leak

A few things help the plumber find it sooner.

Close the main if it is flooding

If the floor is wet, shut off the water at the main.

Check the meter

Turn everything off, then watch if the meter dial still moves.

Mark hot spots

Use tape to outline warm patches on the floor.

Move belongings

Lift rugs and furniture from damp areas.

Code and slab leak work

Rerouting or replacing supply lines follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), including approved materials, pressure testing and protection where pipe passes through walls and framing. Ask your plumber how the new lines will be tested before the walls are closed.
