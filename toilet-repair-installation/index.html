Toilet overflowing right now

Turn the supply valve behind the toilet clockwise to stop refilling, then lift the tank lid and push the flapper down to stop more water entering the bowl. Do not flush again. If the toilet and the tub both gurgle, the problem may be the main line and not the toilet alone.

In that case, read about sewer line cleaning while you wait. If it is only this toilet, a plumber can clear it with an auger and check the flange.

Water that is seeping from the base is the one to act on quickly. A failed wax ring can let water soak the subfloor for weeks without being obvious. If the floor around the toilet feels soft, call.

When the flange is the real problem

The flange is the ring that connects the toilet to the drain. If it cracks or sits below the floor surface, the toilet will not seal and will rock. A plumber can repair it with a replacement flange or a repair ring.

This is not a do-it-yourself job in most cases because the subfloor may be damaged. If the floor is soft, the repair may include a wood repair.

Replacing a toilet instead of fixing it

A toilet that is cracked, that clogs often or that wastes a lot of water per flush is a candidate for replacement. Newer models use less water per flush. Measure the rough-in, which is the distance from the wall to the center of the drain, because 12 inches is common but not universal.

Plumbers can also help if you are remodeling a bathroom. If you are adding a fixture to a new space, see plumbing installation for rough-in work.

Hard use, hard water and short lifespans

The Village of Los Lunas draws groundwater from the Santa Fe Group aquifer through four wells. Minerals in groundwater can build up in toilet fill valves and flush jets over time. Ask the Village for its latest water quality report if you see white deposits.

If you are on a private well in Peralta or other rural parts of the county, mineral content varies by well. A plumber can look at your parts and tell you if deposits are shortening their life.

Toilet installs and the code

The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) that New Mexico adopts sets limits on water use per flush and rules for fixture clearances. A plumber checks the flange, the vent and the clearance around the bowl so the new toilet meets those basics.
