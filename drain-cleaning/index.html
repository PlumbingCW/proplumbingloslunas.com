When a clogged drain becomes an emergency

Most slow drains can wait until morning. A few cannot. Watch for three signs. Several fixtures gurgle at once. A floor drain in the lowest part of the house bubbles. The toilet and the tub back up when the washer drains. Any of them means the blockage is probably in the main line, and sewage has nowhere else to go.

Call right away in those cases and avoid using water until the plumber arrives. Every flush and every shower adds to what ends up on your floor. Our dispatcher can book the sewer line cleaning crew if the blockage looks like it sits past the house wall.

Kitchen sinks, bathroom drains and the main line

The location of the clog decides the tool. Kitchen sinks clog with grease and food scrapes, and a hand-cranked or electric cable usually reopens them. Bathroom sinks and tubs clog with hair and soap film, and a small drum machine with a cable tip handles that. Main lines carry everything the house produces, so they call for a longer cable or a water jet.

A kitchen line that clogs again every few weeks is a different problem from a one-time clog. Grease coats the pipe wall a little more each month, and a cable only punches a hole through it. A scouring pass with hydro jetting restores the full pipe diameter.

Village sewer or septic: it changes who fixes what

Homes inside the Village of Los Lunas generally connect to the Village sewer, which the Village Water Division runs alongside its water service for more than 6,000 customers. If the blockage is in the public main in the street, the Village owns that fix. If it is in the pipe between your house and the main, the repair is yours.

Properties outside the sewer service area, including many rural lots around Peralta, use septic tanks. A septic tank that is full or a drain field that is failing looks like a drain clog from inside the house. A plumber who only cables the line will not fix it. Tell the dispatcher whether you have sewer or septic so the right crew is sent.

How a drain cleaning visit works

  1. You call and describe which fixtures are affected and whether water is overflowing.
  2. The plumber checks the cleanout and the nearest accessible fixture to locate the blockage.
  3. You get a clear price before any cabling or jetting begins.
  4. The line is cleared and tested by running water at several fixtures.
  5. If the clog keeps returning, the plumber explains whether a camera inspection is worth doing.

Cleanouts are the access point that makes this fast. Many houses have one outside near the foundation or in a garage wall. Knowing where yours is saves time, and if you cannot find it, say so on the phone and the plumber will look for it on arrival.

Before we arrive: what to do while a drain is backing up

These steps limit the mess and speed up the visit.

Stop the water

Pause the washer, dishwasher and showers. Every gallon that drains adds to the backup.

Keep people clear

Sewage carries bacteria. Keep children and pets away from standing water and wear gloves if you must touch anything.

Find the cleanout

Look for a capped pipe outside near the foundation. Leave the cap in place unless the plumber asks you to open it.

Do not pour chemicals

Drain cleaner pooled in a backed-up line can burn the plumber and damage the pipe.

Code notes for drain work

The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) that New Mexico adopts sets rules for traps, vents and cleanouts, which is why a plumber checks that your drain has a working vent and an accessible cleanout before and after clearing it. Cleaning alone does not need a permit, but changing drain piping usually does.
