Humming, jammed or leaking: three different problems

A humming disposal usually means the motor is trying to turn but the blades are jammed. A disposal that does nothing may have tripped its reset button or lost power. A disposal that leaks from the bottom or the side has a failed seal or a cracked housing, and no repair will hold.

Most jams can be cleared by turning the blades from underneath with a hex key, but only with the power off. If the disposal still will not turn, call a plumber.

Disposals last for years but not forever. If yours is old and leaking or has been repaired already, a new one is usually the better use of money.

A disposal clog is often a drain clog

If water backs up into the second sink basin or into the dishwasher, the blockage may be beyond the disposal. Food waste, grease and coffee grounds build up in the line after the disposal. A cable or jet clears that. Our drain cleaning page covers the kitchen branch line.

Avoid grease, bones, fibrous vegetables, rice and pasta in the disposal. They are the most common causes of calls we get about jams.

Installing a new disposal

A replacement involves unmounting the old unit, cleaning the sink flange, mounting the new one and reconnecting the drain and the dishwasher hose. The electrical side is usually a plug-in cord or a hard-wired connection. A plumber should confirm the setup is safe.

If you are remodeling a kitchen, ask about the dishwasher drain connection and an air gap. See plumbing installation for other new fixture work.

Kitchen leaks that start under the sink

Water under the sink may come from the disposal, the faucet supply lines or the drain. Dry the cabinet, run the faucet and watch where the drip starts. The location tells you which part failed.

If you cannot trace it, call. Moisture inside a cabinet can warp the floor and grow mold quickly. Our leak detection page covers how plumbers search for leaks in more hidden spots.

Disposals, air gaps and the code

The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) governs how a disposal and dishwasher drain connect, including the high loop or air gap that stops dirty water flowing back into the dishwasher. A plumber checks that connection whenever a unit is replaced.
