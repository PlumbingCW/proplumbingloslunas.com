When a water heater problem is an emergency

A heater that simply runs lukewarm can wait for the next appointment. A tank that is leaking, a gas smell near the unit or a relief valve that is spraying water cannot. Those three need a call now.

If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 before calling us. For a leaking tank, turn the cold water inlet valve a quarter turn clockwise, switch off the breaker or turn the gas control to off, and put a bucket under any drip while you wait.

Why a water heater stops making hot water

Most no-hot-water calls trace back to a short list of parts. On a gas tank the usual suspects are the pilot assembly, the thermocouple and the gas control valve. On an electric tank they are the heating elements and the thermostats. A tripped high-limit reset button also cuts power to an electric unit and is worth checking first.

If the tank itself has corroded through, no part swap helps. A tank that is weeping from the bottom seam is finished. In that case the honest answer is replacement, and our water heater replacement page explains how that decision gets made.

Repair or replace: a simple way to decide

Repair makes sense when the tank is sound and the failed part is cheap compared with a new unit. Replacement makes more sense when the tank leaks, the heater is near the end of its life or the repair cost is a large fraction of a new heater.

Our plumber will tell you which side of the line you are on. If your heater is a tankless unit that throws error codes, the tankless water heater page covers flushing and descaling, which fixes many of those calls.

Water in Los Lunas and what it does to a heater

The Village of Los Lunas pumps its water from the Santa Fe Group aquifer through four wells inside the village. The Village publishes an annual water quality report, and that report is the place to look for mineral content. We do not quote a hardness figure here because we want you to read the utility’s own numbers.

What it means for you is practical. If you see white crust at your faucets or hear popping from the tank, minerals are collecting on the bottom of the heater. A yearly flush slows that down, and a plumber can check the anode rod at the same visit.

Before we arrive: if the tank is leaking or the heater is failing

Four quick steps that protect your floor and your safety.

Cut the water in

Close the cold supply valve above the tank so it stops refilling.

Kill the fuel or power

Turn the breaker off for electric units. For gas, set the control to off and do not relight the pilot yourself.

Protect the floor

Set out towels or a shallow pan and move stored items away from the tank.

Smell gas? Leave

Go outside and call the gas utility or 911 first. Then call us once you are safe.

What the code says about water heaters

The 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) that New Mexico adopts requires a temperature and pressure relief valve and a safe discharge pipe on a storage water heater. A plumber checks both on every repair visit, because a missing or capped relief valve is a hazard no repair should leave in place.
