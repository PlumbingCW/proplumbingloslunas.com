Sewer backup: what to do first

A backup is the main sign that a sewer line has failed or fully clogged. Toilets, tubs and floor drains in the lowest part of the house are the first to show it. The longer water keeps flowing into the house drains, the more sewage ends up inside.

Stop running water, avoid flushing and call. The plumber needs to know whether the backup affects every drain or only one, because that points to the main line or to a branch.

Why sewer lines fail

Sewer lines break down for four main reasons. Roots enter joints and expand. Pipe settles and creates a low spot where waste collects. Old materials such as clay, cast iron or Orangeburg fiber break down with age. Ground movement or heavy loads crack the pipe.

Roots are a standard concern near the Rio Grande bosque, where cottonwoods and other thirsty trees grow. A root that enters a joint can cause a repeat blockage. Our hydro jetting service clears roots back, but a cracked joint needs repair or the roots return.

Finding the problem with a camera

A sewer camera inspection is the step that turns a guess into a plan. A small camera on a cable travels down the line and records its condition. The plumber can see cracks, offsets, roots, bellies and the location of each problem, and measure the distance from the cleanout.

If you are buying a house or have had repeat backups, a camera run is usually worth doing before you decide on a repair. See our sewer inspection page for how that service works on its own.

Who owns which part of the line

In the Village of Los Lunas, the Village Water Division operates the sewer system alongside its water service. The public main in the street is the Village’s responsibility. The lateral between your house and that main is yours. A camera that shows the break location helps sort this out.

New Mexico licenses sewer and septic work under its own contractor classifications, including MM-1 for plumbing and MS-3 for septic and sewer. You can check a contractor’s license on the state lookup page before work begins.

Septic properties around Peralta and rural Valencia County

Homes not on the Village sewer use septic systems. A septic problem can look like a sewer backup, but the cause is a full tank, a failed baffle or a drain field that cannot absorb water. Peralta is an example. The town has no public water system and only about a fifth of residents are sewered, so many lots rely on private septic systems.

That is why the first question we ask on a backup call is whether the house is on sewer or septic. The right crew and tools depend on the answer.

Permits and inspections for sewer work

Sewer repair follows the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in New Mexico, including pipe material, slope and cleanout placement. Work that replaces a section of drain pipe typically needs a permit and an inspection before the trench is closed.
