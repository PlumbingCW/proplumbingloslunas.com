What business owners usually call about

The common commercial calls are leaks in ceilings and walls, backups in restroom lines, water heater failures, clogged floor drains and grease problems in restaurants. Each one affects whether you can open the doors.

A plumber who understands that works toward getting you running first and then fixing the root cause. That is different from residential work, where a cold shower is inconvenient but not a lost day of sales.

Restaurants, grease and the line to the street

Kitchens produce grease that cools and builds up in drain lines. A restaurant with slow floor drains is usually weeks away from a backup. Hydro jetting can clear a grease-coated line quickly, and the plumber can recommend a regular schedule. See hydro jetting for how that works.

Grease traps and interceptors are a separate topic, and local rules can apply. Ask the Village Water Division about any grease management requirements for your business before you settle on a service plan.

Property managers and landlords

If you manage rentals, the questions are about speed and documentation. Who is the tenant contact? Is the water shut off? Is there damage that needs to be photographed for insurance? Giving the dispatcher those details at the start shortens the visit.

Commercial work and the code

Commercial plumbing in New Mexico follows the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), with added requirements for backflow protection, grease handling and fixture counts that depend on the building use. Ask the plumber which of these apply to your space before work begins.
