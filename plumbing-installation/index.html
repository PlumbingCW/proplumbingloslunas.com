Planning a remodel: the order of work

Plumbing happens twice in a remodel. The rough-in goes in before walls close, and the fixtures go on after finishes. Getting the rough-in right matters more than anything else, because moving a drain after the slab or wall is done is expensive.

Remodel plumbing sequence

Slab or crawlspace: where the lines go

In a slab house, new drains may mean cutting concrete. In a house with a crawlspace, lines run below the floor. A plumber surveys access first, because it changes the cost of adding a bathroom or moving a kitchen sink.

If you are adding a second bathroom, ask about the capacity of your existing drain and the water heater. See water heater replacement if the new load is more than the current one can handle.

Permits and code in Valencia County

New Mexico adopts the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code, and the Construction Industries Division issues plumbing permits. The Village handles zoning. A licensed plumber pulls the permit and schedules inspections.

Ask to see the permit before work starts. It protects you at resale.

Fixtures that need special rough-in

Some fixtures need more than a drain and two supply lines. A freestanding tub needs a floor drain placed precisely under the tub. A walk-in shower needs a pan, a waterproof membrane and the right slope. A wall-hung toilet needs a carrier framed into the wall.

Tell the plumber the exact model before the walls close. A fixture chosen late can force the wall open again. If you are still choosing, ask the plumber for the rough-in dimensions of your top two picks.

Planning around your schedule

Plumbing is one trade among several in a remodel. The plumber needs framing done before rough-in and drywall waiting until the rough-in inspection passes. Tile, cabinets and countertops follow, and the finish plumbing comes last.

Share your timeline when you call. A plumber who knows your tile date can schedule the rough-in with room to spare, and the final visit lands after the countertops are set.

Water heaters, gas lines and appliances

New kitchens often add a gas range or a larger water heater. Gas work calls for the right license and a pressure test. If your remodel includes either, mention it at the walkthrough so the plumber can size lines correctly.

See water heater installation and tankless units if your load is changing. Appliances such as ice makers and pot fillers add small supply lines that are easiest to run before the wall is closed.

Plan review and inspections

New plumbing follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in New Mexico. The state Construction Industries Division issues the permit, and inspectors check the rough-in before walls close and the finish at the end. Build those inspection dates into your project schedule.
