When a backup calls for jetting and not a cable

A cable punches a hole through a clog. It does not clean the pipe. If you have cabled the same drain two or three times, the pipe wall is coated in grease or scale and the clog is simply regrowing.

Jetting cleans the wall. A nozzle with rear-facing jets pulls itself along the pipe and blasts the buildup off. That is why plumbers reach for it when drains clog again within weeks or when a main line backs up after heavy use.

How hydro jetting works

  1. The plumber checks access through a cleanout and notes the pipe size and material.
  2. A camera run is often done first so the plumber knows what the line looks like.
  3. A jetting hose with a nozzle is fed through the line while pressurized water cleans the walls.
  4. The line is flushed out and tested with water at your fixtures.
  5. A follow-up camera pass confirms the pipe is clear, if the line was inspected beforehand.

Jetting is not suitable for every pipe. Old clay or cracked cast iron can be damaged by high pressure. That is why the camera step matters, and why a plumber should look before they jet.

Along the Rio Grande valley, cottonwoods and other large trees send roots toward any pipe joint with moisture. Roots are a frequent cause of repeat clogs in older clay or concrete lines. Jetting clears them back, but if the joint is cracked, the roots return. That is when a repair is the honest answer.

Village sewer, private lateral or septic line

Jetting is for the pipe you own. In the Village of Los Lunas, the public sewer main in the street belongs to the Village. Your lateral, which runs from your house to that main, belongs to you. If the blockage looks like it is in the public main, contact the Village Water Division and ask the plumber what they see on the camera.

On septic properties, jetting can help the line from the house to the tank, but it will not empty a full tank or revive a failed drain field. The plumber will tell you honestly if jetting is not the answer.

Standards that apply to jetting

Jetting itself is a cleaning method, not a code item, but it touches the drain system the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) governs. A plumber checks the cleanout, the pipe material and any prior repairs before choosing a jet pressure, because old or cracked pipe can be damaged by too much force.
