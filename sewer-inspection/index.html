Sewer Camera Inspection in Los Lunas, NM.

A sewer camera inspection in Los Lunas, NM shows you what is inside your drain line without digging. A small camera travels down the pipe while the plumber records what it sees. You get a straight answer on the line’s condition and a clear price before work starts.

It is the most useful hour you can spend before buying a house or deciding on a repair.

Three good reasons to inspect the line

  • You are buying a house and want to know the condition of the line before closing.
  • The same drain has clogged more than once.
  • You are planning a remodel, an addition or landscaping that sits over the sewer path.

In all three cases, a camera replaces guesswork. For buyers, it turns a hidden risk into a documented condition. For repeat clogs, it tells you whether the cause is grease, roots or a broken pipe. For remodels, it shows where the line really runs.

What the plumber sees on the screen

The camera head has lights and a lens. As it moves down the line, the plumber narrates what is visible. Normal pipe shows a smooth, continuous wall. Problems show up as cracks, joint offsets, roots coming through seams, scale or grease coating and low sections where water stands.

A good inspection ends with a recording or images you keep. Ask for them. They are useful for negotiations, insurance questions and future plumbers.

Reading the report: repair, monitor or ignore

Not every finding needs action. A slight offset in an old pipe that flows well may only need to be watched. A cracked joint with roots will keep causing problems. The plumber should tell you which category each defect belongs to and what it will cost if you wait.

If the camera shows a problem, the next step is covered on our sewer line repair page. If it shows buildup and nothing broken, sewer line cleaning is the right service.

Inspecting a house in Los Lunas, Bosque Farms or Belen

Houses in the Rio Grande valley range from old farmhouses to recent subdivisions, and the pipe material and age vary widely. A camera is the only way to know for sure what is in the ground. Ask the sellers or their agent where the cleanout is, and whether the house is on the Village sewer or a septic system.

If the house is on septic, a camera can check the line to the tank, but the tank and drain field need their own evaluation. Septic systems are regulated at the state level, so ask the seller for the system’s records.

Why a camera report is a useful record

A camera inspection does not repair anything, but it documents pipe material, slope and defects against what the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) expects of a sound drain. Keep the video and the written summary with your home records.
