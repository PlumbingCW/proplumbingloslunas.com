Start with the question: well or town sewer

Peralta became a town in 2007, and about 3,342 people live there. There is no public water system. Homes draw from private wells, commonly 200 to 400 feet deep, so a plumbing problem often begins at the pump, the pressure tank or the line to the house.

Only about a fifth of the town is sewered. Those homes use low-pressure grinder pumps that send wastewater toward Bosque Farms for treatment. Everyone else relies on a septic system. The first thing our dispatcher asks is which one you have, because the crew and the tools change.

Before we arrive: no water, a leak or a sewer backup in Peralta

These steps protect the house and the pump.

Shut off the pump breaker

If a well line is leaking, switch off the pump so it does not run dry or keep pushing water out.

Close the house valve

Turn off the main valve where the line enters the home to stop water inside.

Stop using drains

On septic or a grinder pump, do not flush or run laundry during a backup.

Well water carries sediment and minerals that a municipal system would filter out. That wears on water heaters faster, so annual flushing pays for itself. In 2013 the New Mexico Environment Department held a well-testing event at the town hall on Molina Road, and a test is still the right first step if the water looks or tastes off.

Grinder pumps and septic: what to do in an outage

A grinder pump needs electricity. If the power fails, the pump cannot move wastewater, so reduce water use until the lights come back. Keep wipes, grease and feminine products out of the system, because they clog the pump faster than anything else.

Septic homes behave differently. Gurgling, slow drains in the whole house and wet ground over the drain field point to the tank or field, not the sewer. We can clear the line from the house to the tank, and the sewer line cleaning page explains when that is the right fix.

Peralta in context

Peralta carries a long history. The Battle of Peralta took place here in 1862 during the Civil War, and many older homes near the river date from the farm era. Older homes mean older pipe, and a house that has had three leaks in five years is a candidate for repiping.
