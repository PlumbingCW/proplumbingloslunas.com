Where Los Cerritos is, and where it is not defined

The village Fiesta Area Plan says Fiesta is the remaining 354 acres of the 531-acre Los Cerritos Area Plan, along I-25 and Los Cerritos Road. The plan calls the site the first impression of Los Lunas for people heading south on the interstate. It also notes land that was previously platted and developed as Los Cerritos, with the Buena Vista subdivision beside it.

The village comprehensive plan lists the stretch from Los Cerritos Road to Sand Sage Road as the village’s fastest-growing commercial corridor. In other words, homes sit close to shopping, and new development is still going in nearby.

We could not find a documented boundary for Los Cerritos as a neighborhood. We describe it by its anchor roads, which are Los Cerritos Road and Highline Drive, and by the subdivision names used on listing sites.

Plumbing guidance for homes in the Los Cerritos area

The area has a mix of subdivisions and a mix of home sizes. We have no verified data on ages, pipe materials or soil here, so we do not say what fails most often. A short visit tells you more than a guess from a map.

Homes near an irrigation ditch or canal should keep roof and yard drainage moving away from the house, and a yard that stays wet without rain is worth a call. A wet spot over a buried line points to leak detection before it points to anything else.

Slow drains in more than one fixture usually mean the problem is in the main line, not the sink. That is a job for drain cleaning, and a camera pass shows whether a sewer inspection is worth doing.
