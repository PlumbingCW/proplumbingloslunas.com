A young city with older neighborhoods

Rio Communities incorporated on May 16, 2013, and about 4,926 people live there. The city is new, but the homes are not. Many were built decades earlier as part of the area’s subdivision growth, so the plumbing inside them has aged on its own timeline.

A house from that era may have steel supply lines or an original water heater. If you are seeing rust in the water or low pressure at several fixtures, ask about repiping before the next leak.

Water from a private utility

Water here comes from the New Mexico Water Service Company, a private franchise. That means billing and outage questions go to the company, not the city. A plumber can tell you whether the problem is on your side of the meter or the company’s side.

Sewer service has been in the news. In 2025 a sewer fee case went to the state regulators, and the city council has been exploring its own system. Rates and ownership may change, but a clogged line in your house is still your responsibility.

Winter and summer, one plumbing plan

Cold nights can freeze exposed pipe, and summer monsoon storms can soak yards and expose weak spots in buried lines. A short seasonal routine helps. Cover outdoor spigots before the first freeze and check the meter box for pooling water after heavy rain.

If a pipe does freeze, shut off the main and call. Our burst pipe repair page lists what to do first.
