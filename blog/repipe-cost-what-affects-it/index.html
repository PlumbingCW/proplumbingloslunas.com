What Affects the Cost of Repiping a House in New Mexico.

The cost of repiping a house is shaped by size, pipe material, how the house is built, permit work and wall patching. We do not publish a dollar figure because the range is wide, and a number without your house in front of it is a guess.

This guide shows what moves the quote so you can compare plumbers honestly.

Material choices

PEX is flexible, resists freezing better than rigid pipe and is common in new work. Copper is durable and familiar. Other plastics have their own tradeoffs. Ask what the plumber recommends for your house and why.

Water quality matters too. Aggressive water can pit copper. If your home is on a private well, test it. Our water treatment page explains testing.

Slab, attic and wall access

In a house on a slab, a repipe often means running new lines through the attic and walls rather than under the concrete. That avoids slab cuts but means more wall openings. Read about the reroute option on our slab leak repair page.

In a house with a crawlspace, access is easier. Homes with finished basements or tile everywhere take longer to patch.

Permits and inspections

New Mexico adopts the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code, and plumbing permits come from the Construction Industries Division. A repipe is normally a permitted job. The fee and the inspection time are part of the project.

Ask whether the permit is included in the quote. If a plumber suggests skipping it, find another plumber.

Repiping in Los Lunas and nearby towns

Many valley houses were built in waves, so the pipe material varies by street. Homes from some decades have galvanized steel, which rusts inside and reduces flow. Newer homes more often have PEX or copper. A plumber can identify the material at the meter or water heater.

In Belen, older downtown buildings can have mixed materials. In Bosque Farms, larger homes may have longer runs. Both make a written survey worth getting.

Repair or repipe: a rule of thumb

If you have had one leak in a decade, repair. If you have had several in a few years, or your water is rusty and weak at every tap, a repipe is worth pricing. Ask the plumber to put both options in writing.

The related read on burst pipe repair shows what a single repair visit looks like, which helps you compare.

How to read a repipe quote

A good quote lists the scope in plain terms. It names the number of fixtures, the pipe material, whether the work is to the meter or to the house wall, the permit and the patching. It states what is not included.

  • Number of bathrooms, kitchens and laundry connections.
  • Water heater connections.
  • Hose bibs and ice maker lines.
  • Shutoff valve replacement.
  • Drywall opening and closing.
  • Paint and texture matching.
  • Cleanup and haul-away.

If a line is missing, ask. A quote without a patching line is not necessarily cheaper. It may be incomplete.

Ways to keep a repipe on track

Plan where you will stay on days without water, and discuss the schedule with the plumber. Move items away from walls the crew will open. Protect floors and ask where the crew will stage materials.

Agree in advance on how changes are handled. If the crew finds hidden damage or a code issue, they should show you before adding cost.

Phased and partial repipes

Some homes do not need every line replaced. A partial repipe replaces the worst section, such as a bathroom group or the lines in one wall. It costs less now but may leave aging pipe elsewhere.

Ask the plumber to describe the remaining pipe and its likely life. A partial repipe makes sense when the rest of the system is in good shape and the problem is isolated.

Water quality and how long new pipe lasts

Pipe life depends on the water that flows through it. Soft or acidic water can pit copper. Water with heavy sediment can wear on fittings. High chlorine levels can age certain plastics faster.

Before you choose a material, ask the plumber to look at your water. The Village of Los Lunas publishes a yearly water quality report, and private well owners can use a lab test. The match between pipe and water often decides how long the new system lasts.

Timing a repipe

The best time for a repipe is before a disaster and before a remodel closes the walls. If you plan a kitchen or bath update in the next year, ask the plumber to coordinate. Opening walls once saves patching money and mess.

Avoid the busiest season for your area if you can. Crews with open schedules can often offer more flexible dates.

Compare, then decide

Collect two or three written quotes. Put them next to each other and mark differences in scope. Ask each plumber to explain the material, the permit, the patching and the schedule. The right choice is the one that fits your house and your plans.

A clear explanation is worth more than the lowest number. Choose the plumber who answers your questions plainly.

Moving in and out during the work

Some families stay home during a repipe, and others stay with friends for a night or two. Ask the plumber what the schedule looks like. Days with water off are usually the first and last, with water on in between.

If you work from home, plan for noise. Drilling, cutting and hammering are part of the process.

Code and the quote

A repipe follows the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and the permit fee is part of the project. Make sure your quote says who pulls it.
