Tankless Water Heater Rebates in New Mexico: What Exists in 2026.

Rebates for a tankless water heater in Los Lunas, NM come from three places: the gas utility, the state and a federal program that is still rolling out. The amounts change, so this guide shows where to look and what to check before you buy.

We do not quote dollar figures that we cannot confirm. Each section points to the source that does.

The short answer

New Mexico Gas Company has increased rebates for storage and tankless gas water heaters, according to its own rebate page. That page does not list the dollar amount in the text we checked, so call the utility or read the current rebate form before you buy.

The state also offers a heat-pump water heater tax credit through the Energy Conservation Program, and a federal home electrification rebate program called HEAR has been announced with a cap of up to $1,750 per household for certain upgrades. HEAR was listed as coming soon when we last checked.

Before you buy: the install matters

A tankless unit is not a swap-in. It needs a gas line sized for a higher burner load, proper venting and often a condensate drain. A line that served a tank may be too small, and the plumber should check it before you order.

The savings you read about depend on those details. If the install needs a new gas line, the rebate may cover only a portion of the project. Read our tankless water heater page for what the install includes.

Hard water and tankless units in the valley

Los Lunas homes on village water draw from the Santa Fe Group aquifer. Mineral scale inside a tankless heat exchanger reduces flow and efficiency, so a descaling schedule or a softener can matter. Our water softener and filtration page explains testing and equipment.

Private wells in Peralta often have more sediment, which is harder on any heater. Ask the installer how the manufacturer treats scale in the warranty.

Rebates in Los Lunas and nearby towns

The gas utility rebate applies to customers connected to New Mexico Gas Company lines. If your home in Peralta or rural Valencia County runs on propane, the gas utility program does not apply, so ask your propane supplier whether it offers anything.

In Bosque Farms and the village of Los Lunas, most homes are on natural gas, which is why the utility program is the first place to look.

Questions to ask your installer

  • Which model qualifies for the rebate and is it on the utility list?
  • Does the gas line need to be upsized and is that in the price?
  • Who files the rebate paperwork and when?
  • Is the unit sized for how many showers run at once?
  • What does the warranty say about scale and water quality?

A good installer answers these before you sign. If they cannot, that is a reason to wait. For a repair on an existing tank before you decide, see water heater repair.

When replacement is the right call

A tank that leaks at the base, rusts or fails repeatedly is a candidate for replacement. If the old unit is failing now, do not wait on a rebate to arrive. Rebates have forms and deadlines, so ask about them, but do not leave a house without hot water.

Our water heater replacement page explains how a replacement visit works.

A simple checklist for claiming any rebate

Rebates are won or lost on paperwork. Keep the itemized invoice, the model and serial number of the new unit and the date of installation. Many programs ask for a photo of the nameplate and a copy of the permit if one was required.

  1. Read the current rules on the program page before you order.
  2. Confirm the exact model is on the eligible list.
  3. Ask the installer to put the model number on the invoice.
  4. Photograph the installed unit and its nameplate.
  5. Submit before the deadline, which is often tied to the install date.

Treat the deadline as real. A rebate that closes thirty days after installation does not wait for a delayed form.

Rebates are not the whole cost story

A rebate lowers the price of the unit. It does not change the cost of the gas line, venting, a condensate drain, a permit or an expansion tank. On some homes those items outweigh the rebate.

Ask your plumber for a line-by-line quote and subtract the rebate yourself. That is the only fair way to compare a tankless unit with a new tank. The related question of whether a tankless unit needs an expansion tank comes up on nearly every install, and a plumber who has checked your pressure can answer it on the spot.

Keep your records for tax time

Even when a rebate is not available, keep the invoice, the model information and the permit. Some credits are claimed on a tax return, not at the point of sale. The state taxation agency can tell you what documents it wants for a given credit.

A short folder in your files with all of it saves time later. Add the manufacturer warranty card and the date of install.

Permits for the install

A gas tankless install is a permitted job that follows the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in New Mexico. Your installer should pull the permit, and some rebate programs ask for a copy.
