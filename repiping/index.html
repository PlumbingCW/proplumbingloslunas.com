Whole House Repiping in Los Lunas, NM.

Whole house repiping in Los Lunas, NM replaces the supply lines inside your walls and under your floors. It is the fix for a house that keeps leaking in new places, delivers rusty water or has low pressure at every tap.

Signs it is time to repipe

  • Repeat leaks in different parts of the house within a few years.
  • Rust-colored or metallic-tasting water when you open the tap.
  • Low water pressure at several fixtures.
  • Visible corrosion, green crust or flaking on pipe.
  • Pinhole sprays in copper.

One repair is ordinary. A pattern of repairs is the signal. If you are seeing it, compare spot repairs against repiping.

Which one you have decides how long it will last and what the repair will look like. A plumber can often tell from the pipe color and the age of the home.

What a repipe involves

The plumber maps the new routes, shuts off the water, opens small access holes in drywall and runs new lines. The old pipe is abandoned or removed. Fixtures are reconnected, the system is pressure tested and the walls are patched.

Most repipes take a few days for an average house. You may be without water for part of each day, and the plumber will give you a schedule. Slab homes sometimes reroute through the attic, which is covered on our slab leak repair page.

Repipe vs repair

If the pipe is sound and the leak is isolated, repair. If leaks are repeating, pressure is low everywhere or the pipe is a known problem material, repiping can cost less over time than a string of emergency visits.

Ask for both options in writing. A plumber who offers only one is not helping you decide. The related guide on burst pipe repair shows what a repair visit includes.

Permits and licensing in New Mexico

New Mexico adopts the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code under its plumbing rules, and the state Construction Industries Division issues plumbing permits. A repipe is normally a permitted job. Confirm your plumber pulls the permit and that the work is inspected.

You can check a contractor’s license on the state lookup page before you sign.

Getting your home ready

Clear a path to every wall the crew will open, including closets and cabinets. Move art, shelving and fragile items. Cover furniture that stays. Tell the crew about pets so doors stay closed.

Plan for water use. Fill a few containers for drinking and cooking on the first day. Ask the plumber how long the water will be off each day and whether the toilets will work at night.

After the repipe

The plumber pressure tests the new lines, flushes them and checks every fixture. You should see stronger flow and clearer water. Ask for a walkthrough, and ask where the main shutoff and any new access panels are.

Keep the permit and inspection sign-off with your home records. It helps with resale and insurance.

The code behind a repipe

A repipe is built to the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), which sets approved materials, pipe sizing, supports and pressure testing. Because it is a major permitted job, ask for the permit number and keep the inspection sign-off with your records.
