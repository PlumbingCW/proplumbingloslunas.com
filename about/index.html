Who we are

Los Lunas Pro Plumbing is based at 1400 Main St NW, Los Lunas, NM 87031. We take plumbing calls for the village and for Bosque Farms, Peralta, Belen and Rio Communities.

We work by phone dispatch. You call, describe the problem and get the next available appointment. A plumber looks at the job and tells you the price before any work begins.

How we work

  • Calls are answered 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays.
  • You hear a clear price before work starts.
  • Same-day appointments are offered when available.
  • Gas problems start with leaving the building and calling the gas utility or 911.

We keep promises we can keep. We do not promise arrival times, because a burst pipe across town can change a schedule. We tell you what we know when you call.

Licensing and standards

New Mexico plumbing work follows the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and plumbing permits come from the state Construction Industries Division. Contractor classifications include MM-1 for plumbing, MM-2 for gas, JP for journeyman plumbers and MS-3 for septic and sewer work.

You should verify any contractor before hiring. The state license lookup is public at rld.nm.gov.

How a call is handled from start to finish

  1. The dispatcher answers and asks for your address, symptom and whether water is running.
  2. You get the next available appointment, with same-day slots offered when available.
  3. The plumber arrives, looks at the problem and explains the options.
  4. You hear a clear price before work starts and decide.
  5. The work is done, tested and explained.
  6. You keep the invoice and any permit paperwork for your records.

There are no forms to fill in. The whole process runs by phone, which keeps it quick when something is leaking.

What we do not do

We do not promise arrival times, we do not quote a price for a problem we have not seen and we do not ask you to sign anything before you hear the price. We do not claim awards, ratings or memberships we cannot show.

If a job is outside our scope, such as a deep well pump pull or major excavation, we tell you and suggest the right kind of contractor.

Safety first, always

Plumbing emergencies are mixed with electrical and gas hazards. Water near outlets calls for turning off the breaker. A gas smell calls for leaving the building. A sewage backup calls for gloves and ventilation.

Each service page lists the steps you can take while you wait. Read the one that matches your problem, and call the line if you are unsure.

The area we know

Los Lunas sits at about 4,860 feet along I-25 and the Rio Grande. Winter nights drop below freezing, groundwater leaves mineral scale and cottonwoods push roots into sewer joints. Those facts shape most of the calls we take.

Our services and blog are written around those facts, not around generic advice.

How we think about pricing

Price depends on the job, so we do not give a number before a plumber has seen the problem. What we do give is a clear price before any work begins, and the reasons behind it. If the scope changes mid-job, the plumber stops and tells you first.

Each service page lists the factors that shape its price. We keep dollar figures off the site because they go stale and because a number without context misleads.

Questions people ask before they call

Many callers ask whether we handle small jobs. Yes, we take faucet, toilet and drain calls as well as larger repairs. Others ask whether we work on weekends. The line is answered every day, and plumbers are dispatched as they become available.

Some ask whether the dispatcher can give a price over the phone. Not for a problem we have not seen, but we can explain what shapes the price and what to expect on arrival.

Working with landlords and property managers

Property managers can call the same line for tenant work orders. Give the dispatcher the tenant contact, the unit number and any access instructions. Tell us if the water is already off and whether there is visible damage.

Photographs of the problem help, and so does a note on who approves the price. A clear chain of approval keeps the repair moving.
