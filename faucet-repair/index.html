Drip or flood: which one do you have

A dripping faucet wastes water and annoys, but it is usually a worn washer or cartridge. A valve that will not close, a supply line that has split or a faucet that has come loose from the sink is an emergency because water keeps flowing.

In either case, the shutoff valves under the sink are your first stop. Turn them clockwise to close. If they will not turn or they leak, close the main house valve and call.

A repair is usually the right move for a faucet in good shape. If the finish is worn or the body is corroded, a new faucet may cost little more than repeated repairs. A plumber can tell you which it is.

Shutoff valves that will not shut

The small valves under sinks and behind toilets are often old and seldom used. They can seize or leak when turned. A failed shutoff valve is a common reason a simple faucet repair turns into a larger job, because you cannot isolate the fixture.

Replacing a shutoff valve is a short job, and it makes every future repair easier. If you are updating a bathroom, plumbing installation is the place to look for new fixtures and rough-in work.

Kitchen sink problems that start at the faucet

A kitchen faucet with a pullout sprayer can leak from the hose connection or the sprayer head. Low flow often points to a clogged aerator, which is a quick fix. Water under the sink is more serious. It can mean a failed supply line or a leaking drain.

If the sink is draining slowly as well, read our drain cleaning page, or if the disposal is involved, see garbage disposal repair.

Cold nights and outdoor spigots

Valencia County sits at about 4,800 feet, so winter nights can drop below freezing. Hose bibs that are not protected can split inside the wall and leak when the weather warms. A spigot that drips after winter may be damaged and not simply worn.

Disconnect hoses before cold weather and cover exposed spigots. If you find a leak after a freeze, shut off the water to that line and call a plumber.

Faucets, backflow and the UPC

The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) requires that faucets and fixtures keep a gap or device that prevents dirty water from flowing back into the supply. That is why a hose-end sprayer or a pullout faucet includes a check valve. A plumber makes sure replacement parts keep that protection.
