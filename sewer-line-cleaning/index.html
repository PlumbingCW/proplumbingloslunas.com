Main line clogs look different from sink clogs

A sink clog affects one fixture. A main line clog affects the whole house at once. Toilets run slow, tubs gurgle when the washer drains and the lowest drain in the house starts to bubble.

The cause is usually one of four things. Grease and soap have built up on the wall over years. Tree roots have entered a joint. Wipes, paper towels or other items have snagged. Or the pipe has sagged and holds water. Only a cleaning plus a camera look tells you which.

How a main line gets cleaned

A plumber opens the cleanout and feeds a large cable machine into the line. The cutting head breaks through the clog and trims roots. For heavier buildup, a water jet follows. When the line is clear, water is run to confirm flow.

Repeat cleaning is a clue. If the same line needs service every year, a camera inspection shows why. Our sewer inspection page covers that step.

Wipes, grease and what not to flush

Wipes marked flushable do not break down like toilet paper. They are one of the most common causes of main line blockages. Grease poured down a kitchen sink cools and coats the pipe. Both are easy to avoid and costly to clear.

Keep a small container for cooking grease and put wipes in the trash. If you are a landlord or manage a rental, a note to tenants helps. These small habits reduce main line calls more than any product from a store.

Village sewer customers and septic homes

If your house connects to the Village of Los Lunas sewer, cleaning the lateral is your job but the public main is the Village’s. If a plumber finds a blockage in the main, they will tell you, and you can report it to the Village Water Division.

If your property uses a septic system, a line cleaning only helps if the blockage is between the house and the tank. A full tank or a clogged outlet filter is a different service. Peralta and parts of rural Valencia County use private septic, so we ask on every call.

A few steps while you wait.

Hold the laundry

No washer, dishwasher or showers until the line is open.

Locate the cleanout

Look outside near the foundation or in a garage wall for a capped pipe.

Protect your floor

Put towels at drains and move items off the floor.

Skip the chemicals

Pouring drain cleaner into a full line risks burns and damage.

Clean-outs and the code

The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) calls for accessible cleanouts on building drains, which is what lets a plumber clear the main line without pulling a toilet. If your house has no usable cleanout, ask about adding one during the visit.
